# Business definitions and data notes
Maintained by the analytics team.

- Revenue: SUM(order_items.sale_price) over items whose status is not 'Cancelled' or 'Returned'. Use order_items.created_at as the sale date.
- Gross margin: sale_price minus products.cost (join order_items.product_id = products.id).
- Costs and expenses: the only cost in the data is products.cost, the cost of the goods sold. There are no operating expenses (marketing, salaries, shipping). When asked about expenses, costs or profit, report cost of goods sold and gross margin, and say that other expenses are not in the data.
- Order: one row in orders, with one or more rows in order_items. Orders and customers are counted over sold items (order_items whose status is not 'Cancelled' or 'Returned'), the same population as revenue: distinct order_items.order_id and distinct order_items.user_id of those items. Average order value = revenue / those orders. A count of all placed orders, whatever their status, must be labelled "orders placed".
- Access: managers may analyse only their configured brands, including all customer spend and churn calculations. Only the CEO has all-brand access. Never provide company-wide totals or benchmarks to a scoped manager. Department and category are analysis dimensions, not access scopes.
- Customer privacy: numeric user_id and spend are allowed, but never associate age, gender, location or other demographic attributes with an individual customer. Demographic analysis must be aggregate-only.
- Customer: one row in users, identified by users.id (= orders.user_id = order_items.user_id). Location is users.state and users.country.
- Statuses (orders.status and order_items.status): Complete, Shipped, Processing, Cancelled, Returned.
- Return rate: items with status 'Returned' divided by all items that were not cancelled.
- Active customer: bought at least once in the last 90 days, using non-cancelled, non-returned items within authorized brands. Apply the user's churn window override to this definition too.
- Churn: by default a customer churns when 90 days pass after a purchase without another purchase within the authorized brands. State the inactivity window and brand scope in every churn answer or report. If the user specifies a different inactivity window (e.g. 60 or 120 days), use that window consistently in the query and active-customer denominator, and state the override. Monthly churn rate = customers who churned in the month / customers active at the start of that month under that same window.
- Churn boundaries: use distinct purchase dates in UTC; a repeat purchase on the threshold date prevents a churn event. Preserve months with no active customers and show their rate as unavailable (NULL), not zero or an omitted month.
- Underspending: a segment's revenue per customer is below the comparison segment or the average across the authorized brands (company-wide only for the CEO). Explain it through number of customers, orders per customer, items per order and average item price.
- Products are described by department (Men, Women), category and brand.
- Quarters follow the calendar year (Q1 = January to March).
- Timestamps are UTC. The current month is incomplete; for trends prefer complete months.

- Monthly churn calculation: the event date is purchase_date + inactivity_window; include events dated within the requested month, and count distinct customers. Do not substitute "no purchase in the 90 days after the month begins": that is a different cohort-retention metric and can use future dates for recent months. Never infer inactivity from an observation window that has not finished.
- Current churn versus historical churn: for "how many customers are churned?" without a period, use the latest valid purchase before the as-of date and count customers whose inactivity window has elapsed as of that date. The default is UTC calendar-date granularity, matching the monthly churn example: compute MAX(DATE(created_at)), not MAX(created_at), and compare DATE values. Do not silently switch to elapsed timestamp hours. For this snapshot, include the exact threshold: DATE_ADD(last_purchase_date, INTERVAL 90 DAY) <= as_of_date (replace 90 with the chosen window), equivalently last_purchase_date <= DATE_SUB(as_of_date, INTERVAL 90 DAY). Do not use a strict less-than cutoff or describe this as "more than 90 days". State the as-of date. A customer who churned and subsequently returned is not currently churned; label historical event counts explicitly as customers who churned during the period or ever churned.
